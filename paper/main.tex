\title{The Window Belongs to the Listener: Allocating Think-While-Listening
Compute on a Device That Pays for It}
\author{Ali Salimi Sadr}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A cascaded voice agent on an edge device answers seconds after the user stops
speaking. The usual remedy, starting the language model on partial
transcripts, assumes the reasoner is idle while the user speaks. On a
unified-memory system-on-chip it is not: the recognizer, the reasoner and the
synthesizer share one memory system and one set of cores. We measure what the
silence is made of on such a device, a Jetson Orin Nano. The recognizer's
final decode is the largest stage on every set we test: \SttShareMulti\% of
the stages after speech on spoken multi-step math problems, against
\LlmShareMulti\% for the language model's first token. We price both kinds of speculation on one axis.
Reply drafts become usable only near the end of the utterance, when little
time is left to finish them, and a draft still decoding at the endpoint costs
\ThinkerOverlap{}. A gated listener, which
commits agreed hypotheses while the user speaks and freezes an orphaned one at
the endpoint, cuts the median paired time to first audio by \PthreeRed{}
($n=\PthreeN$ items). That is our pre-registered prediction, although the
interval's lower end lies below the registered bar. On short utterances the
gate abstains at no detectable TTFA cost. A simulated finite-horizon planner,
calibrated from the logs, beats the gate by a median paired \PlannerDP{} per
item at the gate's duty bound (\PlannerNoDuty{} without it, from decoding back
to back). At the median, the gate is within that of the planner's optimum
under the measured costs. Every prediction, including
those that failed, is reported with its registration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

People take turns with short gaps between them~\cite{stivers2009universals}.
A cascaded voice agent (recognizer, language model, synthesizer) on an edge
device answers much later. On our device, a reactive pipeline answering spoken
multi-step math problems starts speaking a median of \TTFAMulti\,ms after the
user stops (Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}).

A prominent answer is to \emph{think while listening}: start the language
model before the user has finished, so that part of the reply is ready at the
endpoint \cite{predgen2025,ltsvoiceagent2026,epa2026,livemind2024}. Spoken
language models pursue the same idea inside one model
\cite{shanks2025,shih2025thinklisten}. These designs assume compute is idle
while the user speaks. On a unified-memory system-on-chip, the recognizer, the
reasoner and the synthesizer share one memory system and one set of cores. A
speculative decode can be paid for by whatever runs next to it.

This paper measures what the silence is made of on such a device, and which
speculation the listening window should be spent on. The recognizer's final
decode is the largest stage on every length we test. On multi-step problems it
is \SttShareMulti\% of the stage sum, against \LlmShareMulti\% for the language
model's first token (Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}). Speculating the reply attacks a
small stage; speculating the \emph{transcript} attacks the largest. If the
recognizer commits the words that consecutive hypotheses agree on while the
user is still speaking, the final decode at the endpoint shrinks to the
uncommitted tail.

Contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The costs, measured.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item A co-resident process the size of the language model, doing nothing
    but holding pages, slows the recognizer's final decode through
    transparent-huge-page fallback, by a median paired \TaxBallastA{} and
    \TaxBallastB{} in two runs.
  \item A reply draft still decoding at the endpoint delays the first audio by
    \ThinkerOverlap{} ($n=\ThinkerOverlapN$).
  \item A hypothesis still decoding at the endpoint costs the final about
    \ListenerC\,ms per second of overlap, \ListenerCCI. That is below the band
    we registered.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{A gated listener.} Hypotheses are issued only if their words can
  be committed before the expected endpoint. An orphaned hypothesis is frozen
  at the endpoint. Against a baseline that never decodes partials, this cuts
  the median paired time to first audio on multi-step problems by \PthreeRed{}
  ($n=\PthreeN$ items). That meets the prediction we registered, although the
  CI's lower end lies below the registered 300\,ms bar. On short utterances the
  gate abstains, with no detectable TTFA cost (\DevTTFA). The same mechanism
  \emph{without} the gate and the freeze was slower than the baseline
  (\PtwoTTFA\,ms, a reduction CI that includes zero), and our first
  registration of this prediction failed on it.
\item \textbf{An allocator for both consumers.} The allocator logs every
  decision with its inputs. In one logged run on multi-step problems it never
  spent the window on the reply drafter (Table~\ref{tab:allocator}). Wherever a
  draft would have fit, its measured usability was below break-even. Wherever
  usability passed break-even, the registered window model left no room for a
  draft.
\item \textbf{The record.} A ledger lists every prediction with its
  registration commit and its verdict, the failed and the untestable included,
  and with the cases where a scorer or a registration was written late
  (Table~\ref{tab:ledger}\ifextended\else; in full in the extended version\fi).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Speculating the reply.} Cascaded systems start the language model
before the user has finished, through input-time speculation, semantic
triggering, endpoint anticipation or simultaneous inference
\cite{predgen2025,ltsvoiceagent2026,epa2026,livemind2024,ddtsr2026}. Spoken
language models interleave hearing, thinking and speaking
\cite{shanks2025,stitch2026,miniomnireasoner2025,chronothink2026,shih2025thinklisten},
and full-duplex models remove the turn boundary \cite{moshi2024,lws2026}.
Benchmarks evaluate turn-taking and voice agents
\cite{fullduplexbench2025,fullduplexbenchv3_2026,voicebench2026,voiceagentbench2025,evabench2026,provoicebench2026,sid2026}.
Low-latency agent designs, both cascaded and speech-to-speech, are described in
\cite{ilava2025,chipchat2025,kame2026,streamrag2025,moshirag2026}.

\textbf{Speculating the transcript.} Streaming recognition with local
agreement commits the prefix that consecutive hypotheses agree on
\cite{machacek2023whisperstreaming}, on top of Whisper \cite{radford2023whisper}.
Simul-Whisper streams Whisper with
attention-guided chunk decoding and truncation detection
\cite{wang2024simulwhisper}, and other work decides when a streaming
recognizer should emit \cite{x2streaming2026}. We use the mechanism; we do not claim it. What we add
is a measurement of it against reply speculation on hardware the two share,
and a rule for when to issue it.

\textbf{Interference between co-running models.} Co-running neural workloads
interfere on heterogeneous systems-on-chip and on shared accelerators
\cite{haxconn2024,aliyun2018bwlock,kim2024interference,kang2026emcgovernor,sgdrc2025,quilt2026},
and in the datacenter \cite{prophet2017,igniter2022}. The tax we report
involves a co-resident process that does not run at all. Its mechanism is
huge-page fallback in the decoder's own allocations.

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

A turn ends when the voice-activity detector declares the endpoint, a settle
time after the last speech. Let $D$ be the audio the recognizer holds at the
endpoint. Time to first audio (TTFA), from the true end of speech, is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TTFA} = s + F(D - C) + L + S + O,
\end{equation}
where $s$ is the settle time, $C$ the audio committed before the endpoint, $F$
the final decode of the uncommitted tail, $L$ the language model's time to
first token, $S$ the synthesizer's first chunk and $O$ the audio output. The
final is close to affine in its audio, $F(a) = \phi_0 + \phi_1 a$. Two
consumers can spend the listening window.

\textbf{The listener} decodes the uncommitted buffer with a small model and
commits what consecutive hypotheses agree on. It reduces $D - C$. A hypothesis
still decoding at the endpoint competes with the final for the same cores,
adding $c$ milliseconds of final decode per second of overlap, unless it is
stopped.

\textbf{The thinker} drafts the reply from the transcript so far, at fraction
$f$ of the utterance. A draft is usable with probability $p_{\mathrm{usable}}(f)$,
and is worth its cost only above a break-even $p^\ast$. A draft still decoding
at the endpoint delays the reply; the size of the delay depends on device
state. A draft that cannot finish before the endpoint is all cost, so its
decode must fit the window
\begin{equation}
w(f) = D(1-f) - d(fD),
\end{equation}
the speech left after the decode $d$ of what has been heard.

Neither $D$ nor the endpoint is known while the user speaks. A rule has to
judge, for each consumer separately, whether work issued now is
\emph{feasible}: whether it can finish, and for the listener commit, before
the endpoint. For the thinker it must also judge whether the work is
\emph{valuable}: whether $p_{\mathrm{usable}}(\hat f) \ge p^\ast$ at the
estimated fraction $\hat f$. Our rule estimates both from a point prior on
utterance length and from in-pipeline timings.

\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

\textbf{Pipeline.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Silero voice-activity detection inside a Pipecat pipeline.
\item Two Whisper engines, through faster-whisper with int8 weights, on three
  shared CPU cores: \texttt{tiny} for hypotheses and \texttt{base} for the final.
\item A 4-bit Gemma~4~E2B served by llama.cpp with every layer on the GPU, on
  the other three cores, with one slot.
\item Piper for synthesis.
\end{itemize}
Every turn writes a record with its stage timestamps, its decodes and the power
rails.

\textbf{Committing while listening.} A hypothesis worker, a separate process,
decodes only the audio after the committed point. A LocalAgreement-2 committer
\cite{machacek2023whisperstreaming} commits the agreed prefix at segment
granularity, never a word inside the last 0.3\,s of the buffer. At the
endpoint the final decodes only the uncommitted tail.

\textbf{Pacing.} Hypotheses are self-paced. After a decode of length $d$ the
worker rests $(1/\delta - 1)\,d$, with duty $\delta = 0.6$, where $d$ is the
measured length of the last decode. In our tests a duty near 1 split no more
turns than this. The bound is kept because it gives the same outcome with
fewer decodes.

\textbf{The feasibility gate.} A hypothesis is issued only if the decodes
still needed to commit its words, with their rests, fit in the expected
remaining speech: a prior utterance length minus the audio heard so far. The
prior is a point value, the median duration of the set's files, derived at
run start. On short utterances the gate abstains outright.

\textbf{Preemption at the endpoint.} A hypothesis still decoding at the
endpoint is frozen (\texttt{SIGSTOP}). It is resumed once the recognizer is
idle after the reply has played, and its stale result is discarded by sequence
number. The freeze has costs:
\begin{itemize}
\item the resumed decodes still spend energy;
\item a turn that opens while the worker is frozen issues no hypotheses.
\end{itemize}
Two earlier designs failed:
\begin{itemize}
\item Killing the worker and respawning it was falsified as run. By
  elimination, the cause was the respawn landing at every endpoint.
\item Killing it and promoting a hot spare could not be tested, because its
  validity check failed in every attempt.
\end{itemize}
The freeze passed its registered test, with caveats we report with the result
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).

\textbf{The allocator.} For the thinker, at every partial transcript the
allocator estimates $\hat f$ from the prior, looks up $p_{\mathrm{usable}}(\hat f)$
on a curve measured offline, and admits a draft of budget $B$ only if
$p_{\mathrm{usable}}(\hat f) \ge p^\ast$ and $B$ tokens fit in $w(\hat f)$ at
the measured decode rate. The listener's gate runs independently of it.
Every decision is logged with its inputs and its reason
(Table~\ref{tab:allocator}).

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Device.} NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super, 8\,GB of unified memory, in
power mode MAXN\_SUPER with clocks pinned, run headless.

\textbf{Sets.} A development set of 16 short spoken assistant queries, and
splits of Spoken-MQA \cite{wei2025towards}:
\begin{itemize}
\item two digit splits;
\item the multi-step split, derived from GSM8K \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}. We use 80
  items drawn with a fixed seed and, as a fifth length, the 20 longest of
  them.
\end{itemize}
Lengths are in Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}. Spoken-MQA's single-step split served
to select the listener's configuration and to test preemption
(Sec.~\ref{sec:penalties}); it is not in Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}.

\textbf{Protocol.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Audio enters through the microphone's delivery path at real time, one
  file per turn. The next file starts only after the reply has finished and a
  quiet period has passed, so turns never overlap.
\item TTFA is measured from the true end of speech, known to the file source,
  to the first reply audio.
\item In the main comparisons, arms are compared on the same items. Each
  item's value is its median over three runs, the difference is taken per
  item, and the 95\% confidence interval is a percentile bootstrap over items.
  Analyses that depart from this, such as the occupancy tax with one run per
  condition and the overlap fit with intervals over runs, state their design
  with their result.
\item Turns split by the voice-activity detector, turns whose endpoint fired
  after the final, and runs that fail a registered validity check are
  excluded.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Metrics.} TTFA and its stages, the word-error-rate difference against
the baseline on the same audio, energy per turn from the board's power rails
net of the idle floor, and the fraction of words committed at the endpoint.

\textbf{Pre-registration.} Predictions, bars and scoring rules were committed
to the lab notebook before the runs that test them. Corrections are appended,
never edited in. The full ledger\ifextended\else, in the extended version,\fi{}
lists every prediction with its registration commit and its verdict. Where a
prediction was stated informally, a bar moved before its data, or a scorer was
written after the data, the ledger says so.


\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Where the silence goes}
The final decode is the largest stage on every set (Fig.~\ref{fig:stages}).
It takes \SttShareDev\%, \SttShareShort\%, \SttShareLong\% and \SttShareMulti\%
of the stage sum on the development, short-digit, long-digit and multi-step
sets. The language model's first token takes \LlmShareMulti\% on multi-step
problems. We had predicted that the final's share would rise with utterance
length and stay above half. It is not monotone in length and is below half on
two sets, so that prediction is falsified (Table~\ref{tab:ledger}). What stands is that the final decode is the largest
single stage at every length.

\subsection{The occupancy tax}
\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{paper_fig2_occupancy_tax.pdf}
  \caption{Residency alone taxes the recognizer. Final decode, huge pages kept
  and huge-page fault fallbacks per decode, for the recognizer alone and under
  five co-resident conditions: six runs from one instrumented attribution
  session, one per condition, 32 turns each.}
  \label{fig:tax}
\end{figure*}
With the recognizer alone, a final decode takes \AloneSTT{} and suffers
\AloneFallback{} huge-page fault fallbacks; the process keeps \AloneHuge\,MB of
huge pages (Fig.~\ref{fig:tax}). A co-resident process of the language model's
size, doing nothing but holding pages, brings \BallastFallback{} fallbacks per
decode and leaves \BallastHuge\,MB. Paired per turn on the same audio, the
inert process adds \TaxBallastA{} and \TaxBallastB{} to the final in two runs,
and the full pipeline adds \TaxFullA{} and \TaxFullB. Each condition is one
run, made after its baseline. The two runs disagree on the size and on the
order of conditions, and only the second was instrumented for huge pages.
Every interval excludes zero.

\subsection{Two overlap penalties}
\label{sec:penalties}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{paper_fig3_overlap_penalties.pdf}
  \caption{Both consumers' overlap penalties on one axis. Thinker bars and the
  partials-removed bar are TTFA; the listener's in-flight bar is final-decode
  time, the fitted cost per second of overlap times the median overlap.}
  \label{fig:penalties}
\end{figure}
\textbf{Thinker.} A reply draft still decoding at the endpoint delays the
first audio by \ThinkerOverlap{} ($n=\ThinkerOverlapN$ turns), against the same
item's reactive turns in the same run. A draft that finished first shows no
detectable penalty in these runs (\ThinkerFinished, $n=\ThinkerFinishedN$).
An earlier onset test found one contended condition where it did.

\textbf{Listener.} Across the arms that let a partial decode run past the
endpoint (not the freezing arm), a hypothesis still decoding at the endpoint
adds \ListenerC\,ms of final decode per second of overlap, \ListenerCCI{} with
the interval taken over runs (Fig.~\ref{fig:penalties}). That is below the
band we registered, 500 to 1500\,ms/s, and not falsified. Its scorer was written after the data,
and a coding of the arms chosen afterwards gives a higher value. Removing
partial decodes from the reactive pipeline cuts TTFA on the development set by
\PartialsDev{} ($n=\PartialsDevN$ items).

\textbf{Freezing the orphan.} Freezing a hypothesis still running at the
endpoint, against racing it, cuts paired TTFA by \PnineTTFA{} ($n=\PnineN$
items). The final runs at \PnineRatio{} of the solo model of its tail. The
interval's lower end lies below the registered 150\,ms bar. The result holds
under an amendment made before the run; under the original validity rule it
would not be testable. It also depends on a 3\,s quiet period after each
reply.

\subsection{When a draft still fits}

  predicted and as measured.}

The last fraction of the utterance at which a 10-token draft still fits
before the endpoint is \Fstar{} ($n=\FstarN$ items in the interquartile range
of duration). We had
predicted 0.858 (Fig.~\ref{fig:window}). There, a greedy draft is usable with
probability \FstarUsable, within sampling error of the break-even

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{paper_fig5_pass2k.pdf}

Against the reactive pipeline without partial decodes, the gated, freezing

\subsection{Is the gate enough?}
A finite-horizon dynamic program over elapsed time was calibrated from the
logs: decode times, commit boundaries, the final's cost and the endpoint
hazard. In simulation, at the gate's duty bound, the median paired per-item
TTFA reduction of the planner over the gate is \PlannerDP. Without the bound
it is \PlannerNoDuty{}, a gain that comes from decoding back to back, not from
planning. Under the measured costs, the gate is within \PlannerDP{} of the
planner's optimum at the median. Its remaining gain sits in items longer than
the gate's prior. The planner was not run live.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{One device, one model of each kind.} We measure one board class with
CPU-bound recognition. A GPU recognizer would move the stage shares. The
language model is one small 4-bit model, and synthesis is one Piper voice.

\textbf{Audio.} The benchmark audio is synthesized speech, played in at real
time through the capture path. We have no live-speech data yet; one
descriptive single-speaker pass is planned, and it will support no claim.

\textbf{The gate's prior is the set's own median.} The feasibility gate uses a
point prior, the median file duration of the set it runs on. A deployment
would have to learn that prior. On items longer than the prior the gate stops
early. That is where a simulated planner's gain sits, and a prior that adapts
to elapsed speech is an untested hypothesis.

\textbf{The headline's interval.} The gated listener's TTFA reduction met its
pre-registered prediction, but its interval's lower end lies below the
registered bar. The same mechanism without the gate and the freeze did not
reduce TTFA, and our first test of this prediction failed. The listener's
overlap cost per second (\ListenerC\,ms/s, \ListenerCCI) lies below the band
we registered (500 to 1500\,ms/s), and its scorer was written after the data. The share of TTFA
taken by the final decode does not rise monotonically with utterance length,
although we predicted it would, and it is below half on two sets.

\textbf{Costs of the freeze.} Frozen decodes are resumed after the reply and
discarded. They cost a net mean of \StaleMean\,J each over \StaleN{} decodes, and
some drew far more power than their length explains, for reasons we have not
found. A turn that opens while the worker is frozen gets no hypotheses. The
hypothesis worker holds \WorkerPSS\,MB at spawn, before any decode.

\textbf{The occupancy tax.} The tax is measured in two runs with one run per
condition, each after its baseline, so time order is confounded with
condition. The two runs disagree on its size, and the second added
instrumentation.

\textbf{The thinker side.} Draft usability comes from an offline probe of
the 80 seeded items, with no interval carried on the curve. The live comparison of reply speculation is
descriptive: its interleaved design broke at the first voice-activity split,
and its scorer was written after the run.

\textbf{The planner is simulated.} It is calibrated from logs and validated
on the committed fraction. In the calibrated setting, its own interval for the
gate's saving does not contain the value measured on the same items. Commit
boundaries no run reached are invisible to it. We model them with a synthetic
fill whose rate is calibrated on the measured commit fractions. Richer fills
raise the planner's median gain, and so would a steeper final-decode slope.

\textbf{Drift across dates.} Part of a cross-date difference in baseline TTFA
remains unattributed. Every claim-bearing comparison therefore has a same-day
baseline.
