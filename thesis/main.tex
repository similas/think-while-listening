\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{center}
\thesistitle\\[1ex]
Ali Salimi Sadr
\end{center}
% At most 250 words. Written last, from results/RESULTS.md.
A cascaded voice agent on an edge device answers seconds after the user
stops speaking. The usual remedy, starting the language model on partial
transcripts, assumes compute spent while the user speaks would
otherwise sit idle. On a unified-memory system-on-chip it does not. On a
Jetson Orin Nano, this thesis measures what that silence is made of, prices
the two ways of spending the listening window, and builds a rule that
allocates it. The recognizer's final decode is the largest stage after speech
at every length tested: \SttShareMulti\% of the stage sum on spoken
multi-step math problems, against \LlmShareMulti\% for the language model's
first token. Co-residence alone slows the recognizer, through huge-page
fallback. A reply draft still decoding at the endpoint costs
\ThinkerOverlap. On this task a draft becomes usable only near the end of the
utterance, where it no longer fits, and a pre-registered test closed that
line. Committing agreed recognizer hypotheses while the user speaks attacks
the largest stage. With a feasibility gate and a freeze of the
orphaned hypothesis at the endpoint, it cuts the median paired time to first
audio on long utterances by \PthreeRed{} ($n=\PthreeN$; the interval's lower
end lies below the registered bar), and abstains on short ones. At the gate's duty bound,
a simulated planner over the endpoint hazard improves on the gate's median by
\PlannerDP. Predictions were registered before their data; every number comes
from a script over the logs.
